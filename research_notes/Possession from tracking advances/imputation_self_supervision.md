# Imputation, missingness-aware learning and self/weak supervision for trajectory data (2024–2026), judged against a per-frame H/A/D possession classifier with a ball missing on ~41% of frames

Scope note for the report writer: research was capped at about 17 search/fetch calls. The sports-specific multi-agent work (TranSPORTmer, UniTraj, U2Diff/JointDiff, ballradar) and the missing-data benchmarks (TSI-Bench, PYRREGULAR, Perez-Lebel) are covered from primary sources. Autonomous-driving leaderboards (QCNet, MTR successors on Argoverse 2 / Waymo) and crowd modelling were covered only through partial-observation robustness papers, not leaderboards. The "Inferences" bullets are my judgements for this pipeline, not claims from the sources. Context the judgements rely on (from the brief): 25 fps, ~199k frames, anonymous player track slots with identity switches, ball missing on ~41% of frames, 87% of live missing-ball frames in gaps of 10 s or more, where rule-based fills were 13–17 m off; 10 labelled external matches; Apple M2, no CUDA; model frozen for Nov 17, 2026 (5 days from today, Nov 12).

## 1. Time-series imputation 2024–2026 and missingness-aware classifiers: which handle long contiguous gaps, and do any classify directly with missingness?

### Takeaway
General-purpose imputers (SAITS, BRITS, CSDI, ImputeFormer, the PyPOTS family) and time-series foundation models do much worse on block or subsequence gaps than on scattered points. They impute each series from its own history and its correlated channels, so they cannot recover a 10 s+ ball gap whose information lies in the other agents. For classification with missing data, the 2025 PYRREGULAR bake-off finds LightGBM on raw NaN-bearing data in the top statistical tie group, with GRU-D and neural CDEs ranked low and struggling on long sequences. The current GRU + GBT with NaN and flags is already close to the evidence-backed practice, and "GRU-D successors" are not a promising swap.

### Cited Findings
- TSI-Bench (Du et al., 2024) ran 34,804 experiments over 28 imputation algorithms and 8 datasets (air quality, traffic, electricity, healthcare). It is built on the PyPOTS ecosystem and uses PyGrinder to simulate point, subsequence and block missingness. Code and logs are on GitHub. — [TSI-Bench arXiv 2406.12747](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12747v1)
- TSI-Bench states that "block and subsequence missingness patterns result in higher error metrics compared to point missing" and that "point missingness has the least impact on most models". Block missingness causes "more significant information loss over extended periods", and subsequence missingness "further deteriorates model performance". — [TSI-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12747v1)
- TSI-Bench finds that forecasting backbones (e.g., iTransformer, TimesNet) used as imputers can outperform dedicated imputation methods. — [TSI-Bench search summary / arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.12747)
- TSI-Bench, downstream tasks: on PhysioNet2012 with 10% point missingness, iTransformer classification ROC-AUC rose from 0.771 without imputation to 0.852 with imputation (best after imputation: Nonstationary, 0.860). In downstream XGBoost regression on ETT_h1 with 50% block missingness, BRITS-imputed inputs reached MAE 0.826, against 1.413 for mean and 1.496 for median imputation. The classification result covers point missingness only. — [TSI-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12747v1)
- TSI-Bench cost: CSDI (diffusion) took 417.52 s of inference on BeijingAir (10% point missing) on an A100 80GB, and its time grows with the missingness rate. DLinear took 0.17 s and BRITS 17.04 s. CSDI's ItalyAir MAE rose from 0.539 to 4.492 at 90% missingness. — [TSI-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12747v1)
- I could not read TSI-Bench's per-model block/subsequence tables (Appendices D.4–D.5 were past the fetched text), so I have no exact ranking of SAITS, ImputeFormer, CSDI, etc. under block gaps. ImputeFormer was not mentioned in the fetched portion. — [TSI-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12747v1)
- Foundation models: MOMENT is a family of open-source models pretrained with masked time-series prediction and evaluated on imputation (including zero-shot), forecasting, classification and anomaly detection. — [MOMENT arXiv 2402.03885](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.03885v2)
- The MOMENT-1-large Hugging Face card lists the MIT License and ~0.3B parameters (F32 safetensors). Its variants were trained on a single A6000 (404 GPU hours reported). The card gives no CPU-inference guidance and does not describe multivariate handling. — [HF AutonLab/MOMENT-1-large](https://huggingface.co/AutonLab/MOMENT-1-large)
- A 2025 EDF R&D paper describes Chronos, Moirai and TimesFM as forecasting models. It says imputation-specific foundation models remain underexplored, names MOMENT and NuwaTS as the few attempts, and notes that both "rely on fixed-length segments and struggle with irregular sampling or variable-resolution datasets". — [EDF R&D, arXiv 2507.13207](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.13207v3)
- Licences, from a single secondary source that I could not verify against the model cards: TimesFM 3.0 weights reportedly use "timesfm-non-commercial-license-v1.0" with Apache-2.0 code, Moirai 2.0 weights are reportedly non-commercial, and Chronos-2 is reportedly Apache 2.0. — [Beri.net, Oct 2026](https://www.beri.net/article/timesfm-vs-chronos-vs-moirai-time-series-foundation-model-licence-bigquery-sagemaker-cost-2026). Treat as unverified.
- PYRREGULAR (2025) benchmarks 12 classifiers on 34 irregular time-series datasets, including P12, P19 and PAM. ROCKET was the top model overall ("consistent superiority across the datasets"). Its statistical tie group includes BORF, LightGBM, RIFC and TimesNet. RAINDROP, kNN, BRITS, then GRU-D and NCDE ranked lower. — [PYRREGULAR arXiv 2505.06047](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.06047)
- PYRREGULAR: LightGBM on raw irregular data with NaNs, without temporal modelling, ranked among the top ("despite being designed for tabular data, lgbm performs well"). GRU-D and BRITS struggled on long sequences. On P19 after tuning, LightGBM placed 4th, ahead of ContiFormer and GRU-D. On the partially observed datasets specifically, models "designed to handle missing data, such as saits and brits, outperform rocket, borf, and lgbm". Neural methods overall "underperform in these bake-off-style benchmarks". LightGBM trained fastest, and ROCKET is called "the best tradeoff between F1 and runtime". Code is at github.com/fspinna/pyrregular. It wraps aeon, sktime, tslearn, PyPOTS and Diffrax; library licences were not stated on the page. — [PYRREGULAR](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.06047)
- Newer missingness-aware irregular classifiers: Adaptive Time Encoding (NeurIPS 2025) learns latent representations at learnable reference points that capture missingness patterns. SPECTRA (AAAI 2026) targets the coupling of missingness and class imbalance (P12, P19, PAM). WaveGNN works without interpolation. Raindrop (2021) set the standard protocol of randomly dropping sensors at test time. — [NeurIPS 2025 ATE](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/934c0d630a07b3a088e273efcc5731c5-Abstract-Conference.html); [SPECTRA AAAI 2026](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/39682); [Raindrop repo](https://github.com/mims-harvard/Raindrop); [PYRREGULAR search summary](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.06047)

### Inferences
- Generic imputers will not fix the ball problem. Every model in TSI-Bench and every foundation model in this list (MOMENT, Chronos, TimesFM, Moirai) reconstructs a gap from the same series' temporal structure and correlated channels. A 10 s+ ball gap in soccer is not predictable from the ball's own pre-gap and post-gap trajectory (the brief's 13–17 m rule-fill error is the empirical proof). The only usable signal is the other 22 agents, which is a multi-agent problem (Section 2). MOMENT is also channel-independent in its published design, which I did not confirm on the card, and it works on fixed-length windows.
- Bidirectional "bridging" of a gap (last seen ↔ next seen) is what BRITS/SAITS-style models do. The current bidirectional GRU already sees both sides of a gap, so a dedicated imputer adds little unless it brings cross-agent context.
- CSDI-class diffusion imputers are infeasible on an M2 for 199k frames: 417 s on an A100 for a small air-quality set.
- Classification side: PYRREGULAR supports the current design (GBT with native NaN plus a sequence model). GRU-D, NCDE and ContiFormer-type models ranked at or below LightGBM and degrade on long sequences; a match is a very long sequence. Fit: low.
- ROCKET/MiniRocket features on the always-present player channels (team centroids, spreads, velocities), fed to the GBT, are the one cheap, CPU-friendly, licence-light (sktime/aeon) addition with benchmark support. Evidence that it helps this task: none. Usable within days as an experiment, not for the frozen model.
- Licensing: MOMENT (MIT) is clean. TimesFM 3.0 and Moirai weights may be non-commercial (unverified). That matters with commercial partners, but the fit is poor anyway.

### Gaps
- Exact per-model scores under block/subsequence missingness in TSI-Bench (tables not read). No source here quantifies how ImputeFormer or SAITS degrade on very long contiguous gaps (hundreds of steps).
- I found no benchmark of MOMENT/NuwaTS zero-shot imputation on gaps comparable to "≥10 s at 25 fps" (≥250 steps), nor MOMENT's maximum input length on the card (I recall 512 but did not verify).
- PyPOTS licence not confirmed from a fetched source.
- mTAN, ContiFormer and neural ODE/CDE or state-space classifiers were not individually benchmarked beyond the PYRREGULAR mentions.

## 2. Multi-agent trajectory imputation and prediction 2024–2026: inferring one missing agent (the ball) from the others, with errors on long gaps

### Takeaway
This is the most relevant and genuinely different family. Set-attention models trained by masking can infer the ball from players' positions alone over a fully hidden 9.6 s window: TranSPORTmer (ACCV 2024) reports 2.71 m average error on LaLiga data, against 3.89 m for the KDD 2023 "ballradar" model, which itself predicts the ball possessor as an intermediate step. TranSPORTmer also classifies ball state (pass / possession / uncontrolled / out-of-play) through a CLS token. That is close to the H/A/D target, and it reports ~81% state accuracy even when the ball is hidden (my reading of the paper). Codebases are thin: TranSPORTmer's code was not found, UniTraj is Apache-2.0 with weights but CUDA-only (Mamba), and the 2025–26 diffusion successors (U2Diff, JointDiff) are GPU-heavy. This is next-phase work, not days.

### Cited Findings
- TranSPORTmer (Capellera et al., ACCV 2024) is a unified transformer for multi-agent sports. It uses temporal encoders and Set Attention Blocks for social interaction, which keeps the model permutation-equivariant across agents. It covers player forecasting, forecasting-imputation, ball inference and ball imputation, and a CLS token classifies soccer states: passes, possessions, uncontrolled states and out-of-play. — [ACCV 2024 open access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ACCV2024/html/Capellera_TranSPORTmer_A_Holistic_Approach_to_Trajectory_Understanding_in_Multi-Agent_Sports_ACCV_2024_paper.html); [arXiv 2410.17785](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.17785v2)
- TranSPORTmer data: LaLiga 2022–23, 283 matches, 6.25 Hz tracking, sequences of T=60 frames (9.6 s), 23 objects per frame (22 players + ball), split 82,954 train / 7,500 val / 6,258 test sequences. — [arXiv 2410.17785v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.17785v2)
- Ball inference with 100% of ball observations masked over the full 9.6 s window: TranSPORTmer ADE 2.71 m, MaxErr 7.39 m; ballradar ADE 3.89 m, MaxErr 8.79 m. The paper claims "over a 25% improvement in ADE". For ball imputation at 80%/90% masking, ballradar ADE was 0.97 m / 1.51 m (TranSPORTmer's per-variant imputation figures were not legible in the fetched text). — [arXiv 2410.17785v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.17785v2)
- State classification is reported as accuracy only, not F1. With the ball available (player forecasting/imputation settings): 87.35–90.47%. In the ball-inference setting the paper says "the method still achieves an accuracy of 80.84% in state classification" (the fetched summary tied this to the 90% mask setting, so the exact condition should be checked). The "uncontrolled" class is least accurate "due to its challenging subjective nature in annotations". — [arXiv 2410.17785v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.17785v2)
- TranSPORTmer architecture and compute: 128-d embeddings, 16 heads, 512 hidden units per SAB; trained 100 epochs on one RTX A6000, batch 64, AdamW, lr 1e-3. It does not require player velocities for ball inference, whereas ballradar uses velocities. The paper page is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; no code or weights release was found in the fetched text. — [arXiv 2410.17785v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.17785v2)
- Ballradar (Kim et al., KDD 2023) infers the ball trajectory from player trajectories with Set Transformers (permutation invariant/equivariant) and a hierarchical Bi-LSTM. The hierarchy intermediately predicts the player in possession, and a "reality loss" plus post-processing keeps paths physically plausible. Stated applications include filling missing trajectories, semi-automated pass annotation and possession-based metrics. — [arXiv 2306.08206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08206)
- Ballradar errors: position error 3.6886 m for the hierarchical LSTM against 4.9136 m for a Transformer variant. This comes from a third-party summary and should be checked against the paper. — [Liner review](https://liner.com/review/ball-trajectory-inference-from-multiagent-sports-contexts-using-set-transformer)
- UniTraj / "Sports-Traj" (ICLR 2025) treats arbitrary trajectories as masked inputs, so one model covers prediction, imputation and spatio-temporal recovery. It combines a Bidirectional Temporal Mamba encoder with Ghost Spatial Masking in a Transformer, and reports minADE20 improvements over GC-VRNN of 17.9% (Basketball-U), 28.3% (Football-U) and 10.7% (Soccer-U). — [arXiv 2405.17680v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.17680v2); [ICLR 2025 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/03a9a9c1e15850439653bb971a4ad4b3-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- UniTraj repo: Apache License 2.0. Checkpoints (basketball, football, soccer) and raw and pre-processed datasets are on Google Drive. The soccer data come from SoccerTrack (SportsLabKit) top-view scenarios, basketball from Stats Perform, and American football from NFL Next Gen Stats; dataset licences are not stated. It requires mamba-ssm and causal-conv1d with pytorch-cuda 11.8. The README reports no ball-specific or contiguous-block masking results. — [GitHub colorfulfuture/UniTraj-pytorch](https://github.com/colorfulfuture/UniTraj-pytorch)
- U2Diff (CVPR 2025) is a unified diffusion model for multi-agent trajectory completion with state-wise uncertainty estimates. It is evaluated on NBA, Basketball-U, Football-U and Soccer-U and can predict motions of completely unobserved agents. A TPAMI-format extension (arXiv 2605.10717, May 2026, CC BY 4.0) adds a Rank Neural Network that estimates error probability per generated mode. — [CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Capellera_Unified_Uncertainty-Aware_Diffusion_for_Multi-Agent_Trajectory_Modeling_CVPR_2025_paper.html); [arXiv 2503.18589](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18589v2); [arXiv 2605.10717](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10717)
- JointDiff (ICLR 2026, same group) jointly models continuous trajectories and discrete possession events. — [ICLR 2026 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/7ad65363050e550c5234c4365a93442c-Abstract-Conference.html); [arXiv 2509.22522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22522v3)
- Robustness to partially observed histories (driving/pedestrian):
  - Target-driven self-distillation (TSD) randomly masks input trajectories during training to create partially observed branches, used only at training time. The authors note that "methods trained on complete observations exhibit poor robustness to partially observed trajectory inputs". — [arXiv 2501.16767](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16767v2)
  - RMP-YOLO adds a plug-and-play recovery module that reconstructs missing history inside the encoder, and reports that masked-pretraining approaches can leave the decoder weak on incomplete histories. — [arXiv 2409.11696](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.11696v2)
  - An IJCAI 2024 two-stage physics-informed wavelet model reports accuracy with up to 75% missing observations. — [IJCAI 2024 #756](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2024/0756.pdf)
  - A joint pedestrian posterior-sampling model degrades only 8.9% (JADE) and 7.4% (JFDE) at 75% of history frames masked. — [arXiv 2404.00237](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.00237)

### Inferences
- What is genuinely different from what the team tried: a learned, cross-agent, set-equivariant ball (or possessor) inferer trained by masking, rather than hand rules. The published 9.6 s full-mask ADE of 2.71 m (MaxErr 7.39 m) is an order better than the team's 13–17 m rule fills. Caveats: it was measured on clean, identity-consistent LaLiga provider tracking with ~83k training windows from 283 matches. The team has 10 labelled external matches (roughly 8k non-overlapping 9.6 s windows at 6.25 Hz by my arithmetic), anonymous slots with identity switches, and gaps that are mostly ≥10 s (often longer than the 9.6 s window). Expect materially worse errors. How much worse is unknown.
- Predicting the ball's xy is probably the wrong intermediate target. Ballradar's hierarchy, predicting the possessor first, and TranSPORTmer's state CLS head both point to a better idea: predict possession state and team directly from player configurations, trained on frames where the ball is visible and masked artificially. ~81% state accuracy with the ball hidden suggests the players alone carry a lot of the possession signal. That signal is learnable, not rule-codable, and it differs from the "ball-free team-movement features" the team discarded, because it is learned end-to-end with set attention over individual players.
- Free self-supervised labels exist without any human: on every frame where the ball is observed, "nearest player / nearest team / ball-in-play" can be computed algorithmically. That includes the competition match's ~59% observed frames. Masking those frames and training a players-only model to recover them is self-supervision on observed data, not pseudo-labelling of H/A/D. It is arguably outside the grey zone, but the rules should be checked. The key risk is MNAR: the ball goes missing in specific states (aerial balls, crowded boxes, stoppages, camera cuts), so frames where it is observed and artificially masked are not distributed like frames where it is truly missing. Masked-ball training should sample gap positions and lengths to match the target's gap-length distribution, and ideally condition on state proxies.
- Identity switches: per-agent temporal attention (TranSPORTmer, UniTraj) assumes a stable track per slot. With identity switches, use short windows, or frame-level set attention followed by temporal modelling of pooled or team-level embeddings. That variant is robust to slot permutations by construction.
- M2 feasibility: TranSPORTmer is small (128-d, 16 heads) and would likely train on Apple MPS in hours to a day at this data size (my estimate; no source). UniTraj needs mamba-ssm CUDA kernels, so it will not run on an M2 as-is. U2Diff and JointDiff are diffusion models, slow without a GPU. Licences: UniTraj Apache-2.0 (usable). TranSPORTmer has no code found, so it would be a reimplementation, and its paper is CC BY-NC-SA, which covers the text, not the idea.
- Triage: everything in this section is "next phase (weeks+)". The quickest version (days to about 2 weeks) would be a players-only set-transformer head trained on visible-ball frames with gap-matched masking, whose per-frame P(H/A/D) or P(team in possession) becomes a new feature for the existing GBT and decoder stack rather than replacing it. Uncertainty-aware variants (U2Diff-style) are a later step: an explicit "the model does not know" signal could let the min-length Viterbi decoder lean on priors inside long gaps.

### Gaps
- No source reports ball-inference error as a function of gap length beyond 9.6 s, or on uncorrected tracking with identity switches.
- TranSPORTmer's exact imputation numbers (80/90% masking) and whether the 80.84% state accuracy refers to 90% or 100% ball masking need checking in the PDF ([ACCV PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ACCV2024/papers/Capellera_TranSPORTmer_A_Holistic_Approach_to_Trajectory_Understanding_in_Multi-Agent_Sports_ACCV_2024_paper.pdf)).
- I did not find TranSPORTmer, ballradar, U2Diff or JointDiff code/licence pages in this pass. Search results said no ballradar code was found, though I believe a "ballradar" GitHub repo by the first author exists; unverified.
- I did not check the Argoverse 2 / Waymo Open Motion leaderboards (QCNet, MTR successors). Those models predict futures for observed agents and are not designed to infer a never-observed agent, so I judged them lower priority, but this is unverified.
- Licences of the SoccerTrack data and of other public soccer tracking sets usable for pretraining (e.g., Metrica sample, SkillCorner open data, the DFL/IDSSE release) were not checked.

## 3. Self-supervised and weakly supervised learning on trajectories: masked modelling, contrastive pretraining, pseudo-labelling/self-training, programmatic weak supervision, with ~10 labelled sequences

### Takeaway
Masked modelling is the self-supervised mechanism with direct sports evidence: TranSPORTmer, UniTraj and U2Diff are all trained by masking agents or timesteps. The natural masked task here, hiding the ball and predicting it or its possessor from players, needs no possession labels. Self-training with pseudo-labels has a well-documented failure mode, confirmation bias that compounds over iterations, and I found no sports- or tracking-specific evidence for when it helps. Programmatic weak supervision (Snorkel-style) is hard to apply to raw time series. 2024 work responds by propagating a small set of expert labels with distance functions rather than hand-written labelling functions.

### Cited Findings
- UniTraj trains one model on "arbitrary trajectories as masked inputs" to cover prediction, imputation and recovery. TranSPORTmer is trained under multiple masking regimes (forecasting, imputation, full ball inference). — [arXiv 2405.17680v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.17680v2); [arXiv 2410.17785v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.17785v2)
- MOMENT shows masked-reconstruction pretraining yields representations usable for few-shot classification (linear probing / unsupervised representations). — [MOMENT arXiv 2402.03885](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.03885v2); [HF card](https://huggingface.co/AutonLab/MOMENT-1-large)
- A caution from motion prediction: RMP-YOLO reports that masked-pretraining approaches can leave the decoder weaker on incomplete histories, motivating end-to-end training with the recovery module. — [arXiv 2409.11696](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.11696v2)
- Self-training failure mode: a student can overfit to the teacher's wrong pseudo-labels; this bias "impedes improvements in pseudolabel accuracy across self-training iterations, leading to unwanted saturation in model performance after just a few iterations", and biases "may amplify over iterations". — [Enhancing Self-Training Methods, arXiv 2301.07294](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.07294)
- Noisy Student relies on softmax-confidence thresholds to filter low-confidence pseudo-labels. A WACV 2024 paper studies design choices to reduce its confirmation bias. — [WACV 2024, Radhakrishnan et al.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2024/papers/Radhakrishnan_Design_Choices_for_Enhancing_Noisy_Student_Self-Training_WACV_2024_paper.pdf)
- Neighbourhood-regularised self-training is proposed specifically "for learning with few labels" (selection of pseudo-labels using neighbourhood agreement). — [arXiv 2301.03726](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.03726)
- Time-series-specific pseudo-labelling: an ICANN 2021 framework (ResNetIPL) combines CNNs with transductive self-training for multivariate time-series classification with few labels, adding pseudo-labelled samples at each step. — [AMiner record](https://www.aminer.org/pub/6143266c91e011fb3e9ac062/evaluate-pseudo-labeling-and-cnn-for-multi-variate-time-series-classification-in)
- Weak supervision for time series: a 2024 paper argues labelling functions are hard to express for high-dimensional data such as time series. It uses distance functions so that expert labels on a small subset induce labels across the dataset. In a medical time-series case, labelling 50–130 of 3,265 samples improved accuracy by 17–28% and F1 by 13–28% over clinician-defined labelling functions. — [arXiv 2407.07982](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.07982)
- Label-efficient time-series anomaly detection generates labelling functions automatically from a few labels plus active learning, because "raw values of time series are not informative enough for users to generate LFs". — [arXiv 2212.14621](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.14621.pdf)
- Weak supervision has also been applied to wearable sensor classification (Parkinson's) to reduce hand-labelling. — [Papers with Code entry](https://cs.paperswithcode.com/paper/weak-supervision-for-time-series-wearable)

### Inferences
- The team's "~10 labelled sequences" are 10 full matches, about 1.5–2M labelled frames. That is not a few-label regime in the sense of the self-training literature: the scarcity is in match-level diversity (teams, providers, tracking quality), not frame counts. Self-supervised pretraining's main value here is domain adaptation to the target match's tracking quality and missingness, not label efficiency.
- The cleanest self-supervised option is masked-ball reconstruction on all tracking available, including the competition match. Its targets are observed ball positions or algorithmically derived "nearest team" values, not H/A/D. It follows the brief's rule "no person decides any label", and since it uses no possession labels at all it is less grey than H/A/D pseudo-labelling. It still needs a rules check.
- H/A/D pseudo-labelling on the target match is where confirmation bias bites: a model that systematically mislabels long ball-missing gaps would teach itself the same error. If tried, use (a) high-confidence thresholds, restricted mainly to ball-visible frames where the teacher is reliable; (b) one round, not iterations; (c) a noisy student, i.e. pseudo-labels from the full model with the student trained on artificially masked inputs. Option (c) turns pseudo-labelling into a way to transfer ball-visible knowledge into ball-missing frames. That is the self-distillation pattern TSD uses for partial trajectories ([arXiv 2501.16767](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16767v2)). Its value for this task is unproven, and it should be judged only on the 20 SkillCorner test matches, never on competition labels.
- Programmatic weak supervision adds little: the team's ~97 hand-made features already encode the kinds of heuristics labelling functions would (nearest team, stationarity, out-of-bounds), and the evidence says such functions are weak on raw time series. Writing labelling functions by inspecting the competition match would also put a human in the labelling loop, which risks the competition rule.
- Contrastive pretraining for trajectories: I found no 2024–2026 sports-specific evidence in this pass. Low priority.
- Triage: masked-ball self-supervision is next phase (1–3 weeks). One-round confidence-thresholded noisy-student distillation into masked inputs could be prototyped in days but is grey-zone and unvalidated. Snorkel-style labelling functions: not recommended.

### Gaps
- No source quantified pseudo-labelling gains or losses for per-frame sports state or possession classification, or for tracking data under domain shift.
- No 2024–2026 survey on self-supervised learning for trajectories was retrieved in this pass. Contrastive trajectory pretraining evidence is missing.
- Whether masked-ball self-supervision on the competition match is permitted is a rules question, not answerable from the literature.

## 4. Practical recipes: missing indicators vs imputation for trees and RNNs, training-time masking that mimics the test missingness pattern, and gains under distribution shift

### Takeaway
For gradient-boosted trees, native NaN handling (the missing-incorporated-in-attribute approach) plus explicit missing indicators is the benchmarked best practice, which the pipeline already follows. Indicators help because missingness is informative (not at random). That same fact makes them a shortcut that can break when the missingness mechanism shifts between training and target data. The best-supported, genuinely new recipe is training-time masking: drop the ball in the training matches with the target's structured pattern (long contiguous gaps, ~41% rate, state-dependent) so the models learn a ball-missing branch that matches test conditions. Driving and pedestrian prediction papers show that models trained on complete observations are brittle to partial inputs and that masking during training fixes much of it.

### Cited Findings
- Perez-Lebel et al. (GigaScience 2022) benchmarked missing-value strategies with gradient-boosted trees on 8 health databases. Trees that model missing values natively (missing-incorporated attribute) give "robust, fast, and well-performing predictive modeling". Elaborate imputation can beat simple strategies but costs much more time. "Adding an indicator to express which values have been imputed is important, suggesting that the data are missing not at random." — [arXiv 2202.10580](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10580); [PubMed](https://www.pubmed.gov/35426912/)
- The companion protocol states that native support for missing values "predicts better than state-of-the-art imputation with much less computational cost". — [protocols.io](https://www.protocols.io/view/benchmarking-missing-values-approaches-for-predict-b3nfqmbn.html)
- PYRREGULAR found LightGBM with NaNs among the top classifiers on irregular time series and the fastest to train. — [PYRREGULAR](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.06047)
- TSI-Bench: imputation improved downstream classification under point missingness (iTransformer AUC 0.771 → 0.852 on PhysioNet2012). Under 50% block missingness, a learned imputer (BRITS) beat mean or median fill for a downstream XGBoost (MAE 0.826 vs 1.413/1.496). — [TSI-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12747v1)
- Structured missingness: Mitra et al. (Nature Machine Intelligence 5:13–23, 2023) is a position paper. It argues missingness in many ML problems has multivariate structure or pattern rather than being random, and sets out "grand challenges" for learning under it. It proposes no specific masking-augmentation method. — [Warwick repository](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/171321/); [Glasgow eprint](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/321319)
- Training-time masking for robustness: TSD randomly masks input trajectories in training and distils from the full-observation branch; models trained on complete observations "exhibit poor robustness to partially observed trajectory inputs". The posterior-sampling pedestrian model loses only 8.9% / 7.4% (JADE / JFDE) at 75% of history masked, while a competitor "fails to produce plausible predictions under high missing data rates". — [arXiv 2501.16767](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16767v2); [arXiv 2404.00237](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.00237)
- The Raindrop protocol tests robustness by dropping whole sensors at test time, a standard way to measure leave-sensor-out shift in irregular series classification. — [PYRREGULAR search summary / Raindrop](https://github.com/mims-harvard/Raindrop)
- TSI-Bench's PyGrinder library simulates point, subsequence and block missingness patterns, a ready tool for structured masking. — [TSI-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.12747)

### Inferences
- NaN + flags for the GBT matches best practice. Do not replace it with imputed values; if anything, add learned-imputation outputs alongside, never instead (TSI-Bench and Perez-Lebel both show indicators or native NaN beat naive fills).
- The distribution-shift risk is concrete. Missing-ball flags are informative because missingness is state-dependent (e.g., more missing during stoppages or aerial play). If the 10 training matches have a different missingness rate or mechanism than the competition match (~41%, mostly ≥10 s gaps), the GBT and GRU learn a flag-to-label relationship that does not transfer, and the "ball missing" branch is under-trained if training matches rarely lose the ball for long.
- Recipe (days to prototype, retraining required): (1) Measure the target's gap-length histogram and gap-start context: in-play vs dead, pitch zone, player density. These are label-free statistics, so they stay within the rules. (2) On the training matches, artificially blank the ball in contiguous blocks sampled from that histogram until the effective missingness reaches about the target rate, and recompute all ball-derived features through the same NaN + flag path. (3) Train on a mix of original and masked copies, which decorrelates "flag = 1" from any single label and teaches the ball-free branch. (4) Validate only on the 20 SkillCorner test matches with their real missingness, plus the masked external matches, scored with spellF1.
- A refinement is state-dependent masking: blank more often in states where the target loses the ball. The danger is matching the masking to labels (e.g., masking more during dead ball because labels say so), which bakes in a label-missingness correlation that may not hold elsewhere. A safer default is label-agnostic, gap-matched masking.
- For the GRU, masking plus a "time since last seen / time to next seen ball" channel (GRU-D's decay idea, implemented as features) is the cheap equivalent. The bidirectional GRU can see gap edges, so a feature like "which team was nearest at the last and next sighting, weighted by distance in time" is likely already implicit. Worth checking whether it is explicit.
- The model is frozen for Nov 17, so none of this changes the submission. All of it is post-deadline work, and gap-matched masking retraining is the first thing to run after the deadline.

#### Consolidated triage (my judgement; evidence quality in brackets)

Usable within days, after the freeze, on an M2:
- Gap-matched structured ball masking of the training matches for both the GRU and the GBT, using the target's label-free gap statistics [moderate: robustness gains shown in trajectory prediction (TSD, posterior sampling); untested for possession classification].
- Explicit gap-context features (time since/to the ball's last/next sighting, nearest team at gap edges, gap length) if not already present [low–moderate: GRU-D-style reasoning; no direct benchmark].
- MiniRocket/ROCKET features on always-present player channels into the GBT [moderate for generic time-series classification (PYRREGULAR); no evidence for this task; libraries believed permissive (sktime/aeon), licence not verified here].
- One-round, high-confidence noisy-student distillation (teacher on full inputs, student on ball-masked inputs) [low: general self-training evidence only; grey zone if run on the competition match].

Next phase (weeks+):
- A players-only set-attention possession/state head (TranSPORTmer/ballradar-style, frame-level sets to tolerate identity switches), trained by masked-ball self-supervision on visible-ball frames, whose output feeds the existing GBT, decoder and post stages [moderate: peer-reviewed ACCV 2024 / KDD 2023 results on clean data, 2.71 m ADE and ~81% state accuracy with the ball hidden; no code found for TranSPORTmer; reimplementation needed; MPS-feasible by my estimate].
- Uncertainty-aware completion (U2Diff) or joint trajectory + possession-event modelling (JointDiff) to give the decoder calibrated "unknown" signals [moderate peer-reviewed (CVPR 2025 / ICLR 2026); GPU-heavy diffusion; code/licence not verified].
- UniTraj pretrained checkpoints [Apache 2.0, weights available; requires CUDA mamba-ssm, trained on SoccerTrack top-view data with a large domain gap; low fit on M2].

Not recommended:
- Generic imputers (CSDI, SAITS, ImputeFormer), because they cannot use cross-agent context for 10 s+ gaps and diffusion is too slow.
- Time-series foundation models (MOMENT, Chronos, TimesFM, Moirai): mostly univariate or forecast-oriented, fixed windows, and some weights are reportedly non-commercial.
- GRU-D, NCDE or ContiFormer as replacement classifiers, which rank at or below LightGBM in PYRREGULAR and struggle on long sequences.
- Snorkel-style labelling functions, weak on raw time series and they risk human-in-the-loop labelling of the competition match.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 source directly measures missingness-augmentation gains for classification under a shift in the missingness mechanism. The trajectory-prediction evidence (TSD, posterior sampling) is about regression error, not labels.
- No source compares missing-indicator vs imputation for RNNs specifically under shift. Perez-Lebel's evidence is for trees on health data within distribution.
- Licences for sktime/aeon/PyPOTS/LightGBM were not verified from fetched sources in this pass.
- Whether the 10 external training matches have a missingness profile similar to the competition match (~41%, long gaps) is a project-data question; the size of the shift determines how much the masking recipe matters.

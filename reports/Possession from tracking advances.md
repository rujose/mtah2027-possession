# Possession research converges on masking the ball

No paper from 2025–2026 offers a drop-in gain for the frozen model before the 17 November deadline. PathCRF, now peer-reviewed at KDD 2026, is still the only open, pretrained model that infers possession without the ball. Nobody has published a successor to Lang et al. 2022 for dead ball: FIFA's own event pipeline takes live/dead status from the data provider instead of inferring it. The real advance is a change of method. In soccer, basketball and American football, the strongest models now treat the ball as one more agent. They hide it during training and recover it, or the possession state, from the players with permutation-invariant (set) attention. TranSPORTmer recovers a ball hidden for a full 9.6 s window to 2.71 m. The team's interpolation-based fills were 13–17 m off in long gaps. Diffusion successors (U2Diff, JointDiff) add per-frame uncertainty and discrete possession events. Two new open datasets (SoccerTrack v2 and the ELASTIC touch benchmark, both CC BY 4.0) give cleaner labels. Outside sport, the best evidence on missing data, segment decoding and domain shift mostly endorses choices the pipeline has already made: gradient-boosted trees with native NaN handling and missing flags, damage matched to the target's gap lengths, duration constraints rather than transition priors, and label-free checks used as alarms. The same evidence rules out test-time adaptation, generic imputers and time-series foundation models. Until the deadline, then, the useful work is on evaluation: a touch-timing audit, two more label-free alarms and possibly one more untouched test set. The next phase should start with a players-only set encoder trained by masking the ball, plus more realistic damage. None of these methods has been tested on segment F1 with identity switches, so the size of any gain is unknown.

## PathCRF still leads, and nobody has replaced Lang's dead-ball model

The most useful result of the search is a negative one. No 2025–2026 paper infers team possession without the ball better than PathCRF, which the pipeline already uses as a feature. It is now a KDD 2026 paper ([ACM](https://doi.org/10.1145/3770855.3818152)). Its repository has had no new weights since the team's survey (MPL-2.0, last push 2026-06-08) ([GitHub](https://github.com/hyunsungkim-ds/pathcrf)). The published v2 keeps the limits that matter here. Its **92.40 % frame accuracy comes from one clean test match after training on five**. It runs only on in-play episodes, on the grounds that "no on-ball events occur during stoppages". Out-of-play is modelled only as four absorbing "outside" nodes. It assumes 22 continuous players, and the main text has no noise or identity-switch experiment ([arXiv 2602.12080v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12080v2)). Peral et al.'s masked multimodal model is now a WACV 2026 paper. Its numbers are unchanged (**85.85 % ball state, 89.56 % possessor**) and there is still no code ([CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2026/html/Peral_Multi-Modal_Soccer_Scene_Analysis_with_Masked_Pre-Training_WACV_2026_paper.html)).

Dead ball is a starker case. No 2025–2026 paper classifies ball-in-play against stoppage from player positions alone. The newest soccer models that segment matches into phases take possession as an *input*. Li & Link's temporal graph attention network segments in-possession phases on 7 Bundesliga matches with possession given ([arXiv 2606.09289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09289)). Renner et al.'s GNN-LSTM labels 7 match phases across 203 matches with possession and ball-status indicators as inputs ([arXiv 2610.11571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11571)). FIFA's automatic event detection from broadcast-derived tracking (Mills et al., *Sports Engineering* 2026) also takes **a frame-by-frame live/dead status as an input** and flags set pieces at dead-to-live switches ([SHURA](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/37410/)). The related last-touch system for out-of-bounds decisions needs skeletal and ball tracking, and gets up to **82.5 %** of restart-possession calls right ([LIDA](https://lida.sport-iat.de/dfb/Record/4094620)). So the pipeline's learned dead-ball class and its per-match stoppage switch go beyond anything published. This also means no external yardstick exists for them.

Two new datasets measure what players alone can do, and both support the pipeline's split of labour. SoccerTrack v2 gives 10 university matches at 25 fps under CC BY 4.0. Its trajectory-only baseline, a temporal convolutional network (TCN) at 5 Hz, spots ball actions within 1 s at **mAP 0.222 without the ball against 0.599 with it**. Within 5 s the gap narrows to **0.524 against 0.666**, and "Out" rises from 0.148 to 0.364 when the ball is added ([arXiv 2508.01802v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.01802v2)). Players carry segment-level evidence; the ball carries boundary timing. The pipeline already works this way: player-based probabilities set the segments, and seen touches place the boundaries in the "timing" stage. SoccerNet-GAR shows that restart types are visible in player layout even with a third of the ball gone. A position-only, role-aware graph network reaches **77.8 % balanced accuracy against 60.9 % for the best video model, with 180K parameters against 86.3M** ([arXiv 2511.12606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.12606)). The first version reported 85.7 % on "Out" and 86.5 % on free kicks, with the ball missing on **32.4 %** of frames ([arXiv 2511.12606v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.12606v1)). Its data is gated under an "other" licence and its code has no licence ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/OpenSportsLab/SoccerNet-GAR)), so it can supply ideas but not training data.

The SoccerNet challenges have moved away from this problem. The 2025 Team Ball Action Spotting winner re-implemented T-DEED and reached 60.03 Team-mAP@1 without any tracking input ([arXiv 2508.19182](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19182)). The 2026 edition dropped game-state reconstruction and team action spotting. It introduced Player-Centric Ball Action Spotting on FOOTPASS (who did what, won at 58.94 Macro-F1) and ball action anticipation ([arXiv 2607.07320](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.07320v1)). Only two ideas transfer. The runner-up, PC-SSAS, fed tracking rows, ball detections and **possession heuristics** into a state-space model. Intellindust's "team alignment" step corrects an action's team from the preceding related action, which parallels the pipeline's "segments" stage ([arXiv 2607.07320](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.07320v1); [arXiv 2508.19182](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19182)). Published data-quality figures also put the competition match in context. PFF's manually refined broadcast tracking still misses the ball on 32.4 % of frames ([arXiv 2511.12606v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.12606v1)). One SkillCorner open match extrapolates 17.7 % of ball positions ([kloppy #639](https://github.com/PySport/kloppy/issues/639)). **At about 41 % missing, the competition match is harsher than any published dataset the search found.** A 2024 PLOS ONE study observed that in Metrica's data **the ball position is typically equal to the possessor's position** ([PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0298107)). That artefact is worth checking on the three Metrica samples, because it would make a "seen" ball partly an echo of the tracker's own possession guess.

## Masking the ball turns it into one more agent to infer

The real movement in 2024–2026 is in multi-agent trajectory modelling, where the ball is one agent among 23. It is hidden during training and inferred from the rest. TranSPORTmer was already in the survey. What has changed is the team's own measurement of what rules can do. With the ball hidden for an entire 9.6 s window, TranSPORTmer infers it at **2.71 m mean error (7.39 m maximum) against BallRadar's 3.89 m**. It was trained on 283 LaLiga matches at 6.25 Hz. Its classification token labels each frame as pass, possession, uncontrolled or out-of-play: 87–90 % correct with the ball available, and a reported **80.84 %** in the ball-inference setting. The exact masking condition behind that last figure still needs checking in the PDF ([arXiv 2410.17785v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.17785v2)).

The team's discarded `ballfill` candidate (`docs/lab/ballfill.md`) gives the comparison. 87 % of live missing-ball frames sit in gaps of 10 s or more, where interpolation plus a learned offset is 13–17 m off. The predicted ball's nearest player belongs to the team in possession barely more often than a coin flip. The comparison is not like for like: TranSPORTmer had clean provider tracking, about 83,000 training windows and gaps of at most 9.6 s. Still, a learned cross-agent model is a different class of tool from interpolation, and the published results favour predicting the possession state over predicting the ball's coordinates. BallRadar predicts the possessor first and the ball second ([arXiv 2306.08206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08206)).

The 2025–2026 successors add uncertainty and discrete state. U2Diff (CVPR 2025) completes multi-agent trajectories with per-state uncertainty and can generate agents that were never observed ([CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Capellera_Unified_Uncertainty-Aware_Diffusion_for_Multi-Agent_Trajectory_Modeling_CVPR_2025_paper.html)). Its TPAMI extension adds per-agent, per-step heteroscedastic uncertainty, meaning each prediction gets its own error spread, and a network that ranks generated modes by error probability ([arXiv 2605.10717](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10717)). **JointDiff (ICLR 2026) jointly generates continuous trajectories and discrete possession events** ([ICLR 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/7ad65363050e550c5234c4365a93442c-Abstract-Conference.html)). It is the closest any 2026 paper comes to the H/A/D target. It is also a diffusion model, slow without a GPU, and its code and licence were not verified. UniTraj (ICLR 2025) is the only one with public checkpoints, under Apache-2.0. But it was trained on Stats Perform, NFL Next Gen Stats and SoccerTrack data with no stated data terms, it needs CUDA-only Mamba kernels, and it has no possession head ([GitHub](https://github.com/colorfulfuture/UniTraj-pytorch)). Its follow-up also warns against borrowing other sports: **training on all three sports was worse than training per sport**, with Soccer-U error 104.66 against 91.52 for AdaSports-Traj ([arXiv 2509.16095](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16095)).

Two masked models fit the team's specific damage, anonymous slots and broken tracks. T-MAE (*Machine Learning*, 2025) masks **whole individual agent trajectories** in a transformer that attends over time and over agents in turn. It is permutation-equivariant in agents and is aimed at classifying tracking data from few labelled sequences. No code is linked and its numbers were not retrievable ([ML Anthology](https://mlanthology.org/mlj/2025/rudolph2025mlj-masked)). MIDAS combines a Set Transformer with per-player bidirectional LSTMs. On the Metrica samples, under uniformly scattered missing frames, it imputes player positions at **1.32 against 2.09 for ImputeFormer**. It handles players only, not the ball, and its repository has no licence ([arXiv 2408.10878](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.10878); [GitHub](https://github.com/gkswns95/midas)).

On architecture, the most informative 2026 result comes from Li & Link's phase segmentation. The temporal model does most of the work. At the intention level their temporal graph network and a plain transformer tie at **0.74** temporal IoU (overlap in time between predicted and true segments), ahead of a graph network alone (0.71), a random forest (0.62) and rules (0.59). The graph adds 0.03 at the finer phase level. They also use a post-filter that reassigns runs shorter than 10–50 frames ([arXiv 2606.09289](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09289)). This independently supports the pipeline's min-length Viterbi. **No paper compares a set or graph encoder with a GRU or a TCN on per-frame possession scored by segment F1**, so the gain over the current bidirectional GRU (BiGRU) is unmeasured.

The other sports explain why soccer has to solve this alone. Basketball, the NHL and the NFL track the ball or puck directly. The NHL tracks the puck at 60 Hz with sensors ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/nhl/story/_/id/38724195/nhl-brings-advanced-puck-tracking-stats-public)), and hockey research takes possession from the league's own feed ([UWSpace](https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/items/20229aef-d769-468e-9f51-fe1ae8f094f6)). The only new basketball "who holds the ball" result, TacticExpert, scores macro-F1 0.83 on 1,780 images with a 7B-parameter language model ([arXiv 2503.10722](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.10722v1)). What does transfer is small-data practice. The NFL's Expected Rushing Yards model came from "The Zoo", an **order-invariant pairwise defender × attacker tensor** that ignores player order, which suits anonymous slots ([NFL Operations](https://operations.nfl.com/gameday/analytics/stats-articles/what-the-top-predictions-looked-like-in-the-nfls-big-data-bowl)). The 2026 Big Data Bowl prediction winner credited rotation augmentation, an auxiliary loss and a Gaussian likelihood loss for beating overfitting on limited data ([Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/chack3)), and TacticAI found reflection symmetry "a clear advantage" ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10951310/)). Handball shows that a small labelled set can be enough: FAUPA-ML labelled only **10 games** and then segmented attacks across **539 matches** ([IAT record](https://www.iat.uni-leipzig.de/datenbanken/iks/dbv-base-softball/Record/4085680)), the same scale as the team's 10 labelled matches.

## Outside sport, the evidence mostly endorses the pipeline's existing choices

Generic imputation will not fix a ball that goes missing for 10 s or more. TSI-Bench ran 34,804 experiments over 28 imputers and found that **block and subsequence gaps hurt far more than scattered points** ([arXiv 2406.12747](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12747v1)). Those models rebuild a gap from the same series and its correlated channels. Here, the information about a long ball gap lies in the 22 other agents. Diffusion imputers are also costly: CSDI took 417 s on an A100 for a small air-quality set ([arXiv 2406.12747](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12747v1)). Foundation models for imputation remain "underexplored" and rely on fixed-length segments ([arXiv 2507.13207](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.13207v3)).

For classifying data with gaps, the 2025 PYRREGULAR comparison of 12 classifiers on 34 irregular time-series datasets placed **LightGBM on raw NaN-bearing data in the top statistical tie group**. GRU-D and neural controlled differential equations (NCDE) ranked lower and struggled on long sequences ([arXiv 2505.06047](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.06047)). In a separate benchmark on health data, Perez-Lebel et al. found that trees that handle missing values natively, plus missing-indicator flags, are the robust default, because the data are missing not at random ([arXiv 2202.10580](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10580)). v13's robust model (gradient-boosted trees on blanked features) and its GRU (missing set to 0, plus 44 flags) already follow this design (`docs/models/v13-temporal.md`). Swapping to GRU-D-style successors has no support.

Training-time masking has support from neighbouring fields. Driving and pedestrian prediction papers find that models trained on complete observations are brittle to partial inputs, and that masking during training fixes much of the problem. One pedestrian model loses only 8.9 % (joint average displacement error) with 75 % of its history hidden ([arXiv 2501.16767](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16767v2); [arXiv 2404.00237](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.00237)). Sensor dropout during training improves robustness on Earth-observation series ([arXiv 2407.15512](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.15512v2)). A NeurIPS 2025 paper argues that real faults are *structured* (bias, drift, hard faults), which generic augmentation misses ([NeurIPS 2025](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/e43ac0bc7daa066aa6861e2c04d5dfdc-Paper-Conference.pdf)). The team's bench already matches the target's gap structure closely (`docs/bench.md`):

| | bench | competition match |
|---|---|---|
| ball missing overall | 41.0 % | 40.7 % |
| median gap length | 10.8 s | 10.7 s |
| 90th-percentile gap length | 34.8 s | 34.2 s |
| ball missing while players stand still | 65.8 % | 83.0 % |

Its own list of faults it does not imitate yet (false ball detections, identity swaps inside a track piece, one player in two slots) is exactly the structured-fault territory that paper points to. The last row of the table is also unmatched: on the bench, a missing ball is a weaker hint of dead ball than on the competition match.

Decoding research gives the same answer from a different angle. Constraint-Aware Decoding (CAD, May 2026) adds hard limits on durations, allowed transitions and allowed start and end states to Viterbi. It gains 2.5 F1@50 on a weak backbone (ASFormer on the Breakfast benchmark) but **loses 1.4 on a strong one (FACT)**. A Viterbi with transition priors only scored *below* no decoding at all in one 50Salads setting (30.1 against 37.0 F1@50). The backbone matters more: FACT beats ASFormer by about 11 F1@50 on Breakfast ([arXiv 2605.10149](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10149)). The team's own explicit-duration hidden semi-Markov decoder was discarded on the bench (`docs/lab/hsmm.md`), which fits this pattern: duration bounds help noisy per-frame models, and gains shift to the per-frame model once decoding is adequate. Licences narrow the backbone choices: FACT is MIT, LTContext is CC BY-NC, and BaFormer has no licence ([GitHub FACT](https://github.com/ZijiaLewisLu/CVPR2024-FACT); [LTContext](https://github.com/LTContext/LTContext); [BaFormer](https://github.com/peiyao-w/BaFormer)).

The domain-shift literature sets the clearest limits for a single unlabelled match. Entropy-minimising test-time adaptation (TENT and its relatives) fails on time-correlated streams. In the TTAB benchmark, TENT exceeded **76 % error** in one non-stationary setting and "still fail[s]" even with good model selection ([PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/zhao23d/zhao23d.pdf)). Standard adaptation "consistently hurt" in correlated video ([arXiv 2303.10536](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.10536)). Gradient-boosted trees have no parameters to adapt this way. EM-based prior correction with bias-corrected temperature scaling is "hard to beat" for label shift ([arXiv 1901.06852](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.06852)), but it assumes p(features | label) stays the same, and a new tracker breaks that assumption. Unsupervised domain adaptation for time series adds about **+0.05 to +0.08 accuracy** at best, and several methods fall below training on the source alone. Choosing a model without target labels was **significantly worse than choosing with them** (for example 0.59–0.60 against 0.70) ([arXiv 2312.09857v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.09857v3)). Agreement-on-the-line shows that agreement between models tracks their accuracy across a family of neural networks ([arXiv 2206.13089](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.13089)). **No method found estimates segment F1 without labels.**

## Before 17 November, only the measuring stick should change

With the model frozen, the 2025–2026 work can help only with evaluation and documentation. The highest-value job is a timing audit with the ELASTIC benchmark, now a CIKM 2026 paper. Three annotators re-annotated three Sportec matches frame by frame, and the team's bench already contains all three (J03WMX, J03WN1, J03WPY). Each event has an event frame (`frame_id`) and a ball-reception frame (`receive_frame_id`), released under CC BY 4.0 with MPL-2.0 code ([ELASTIC](https://github.com/hyunsungkim-ds/elastic); [arXiv 2608.30227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30227)). Comparing DFL's per-frame possession changes and v13's changes with these reception frames shows two things: whether the bench's own labels lag the true touch, and so whether the timing stage learned a provider offset rather than a touch rule. It takes half a day to a day. It is a check on label precision, not an independent test, and it belongs in the technical document's limitations.

The second job extends the existing label-free checks (`bench/transfer_check.py`, run by Jose and printing only PASS/NEAR/WARN). The first new check is agreement spellF1 between variants the pipeline already computes: the GRU start, the robust start, their average, and branches A and B. This is a segment-level version of agreement-on-the-line. The second is a D share re-estimated by EM prior correction, compared with the bench's range. Both should only *veto* an output that looks anomalous, never select between candidates, given the large gap between label-free and oracle selection.

The literature's favourite shift diagnostic is a domain classifier trained to tell bench frames from competition frames. RULES.md permits unsupervised learning on the provided game's inputs, but the team has chosen to treat analyses of that kind as off-limits (`docs/field.md`). That is a team policy to keep or revisit deliberately, not something to slip in now.

The third job is optional. SoccerTrack v2 could become a third untouched test set. It has team-attributed events, a per-frame ball-status flag, CC BY 4.0 data and an auto-approved gated download ([LICENSE-DATA](https://github.com/AtomScott/SoccerTrack-v2/blob/main/LICENSE-DATA); [HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/atomscott/soccertrack-v2)). Its ball, however, is drawn as straight lines between annotated events ([arXiv 2508.01802v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.01802v2)). After the bench's damage it would test a shift in level of play and labelling, not a tracker shift. At 2–3 days it is borderline, and is worth doing only if the loader is quick. It also gives the technical document something to cite: PathCRF's and Peral's new venues, the absence of any published dead-ball successor, and SoccerTrack v2's 0.22-against-0.60 result as outside support for "players for segments, ball for boundaries".

Several tempting moves should wait or not happen at all. Test-time adaptation fails on correlated streams. Pseudo-labelling the competition match is allowed if no person decides the labels, but confirmation bias compounds over self-training iterations ([arXiv 2301.07294](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.07294)), and there is no time to validate it. Mirror test-time averaging and EM-corrected decoding both change the submitted output, so they belong to the next phase however cheap they look. No foundation model fits at all.

## After the deadline, a masked-ball set encoder heads the queue

The next phase should invest where the literature and the team's measured weaknesses meet. Three signals point the same way: the **cross-tracker drop** from 0.716 on the bench to 0.408 on SkillCorner (`docs/models/v13-temporal.md`); the **cost of a missing ball**, with the robust model calling the team right 93 % of the time when the ball is seen and 86 % when it is not (`docs/lab/ballfill.md`); and the bench's known gaps in damage realism described above. The table ranks the candidates by fit to those gaps, not by headline benchmark numbers.

| Rank | What to build | Why the 2025–26 evidence points here | Cost, licence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A players-only set-attention head that predicts P(team in possession) or H/A/D, trained by masking the seen ball with gaps drawn from the target's gap-length distribution. Use per-frame sets, so identity switches do not break it. Feed its output to the trees and the decoder; do not replace them | TranSPORTmer: 2.71 m and ~81 % state accuracy with the ball hidden; BallRadar's possessor-first design; JointDiff's joint possession events. RULES.md allows self-supervision on the provided game's ~59 % seen-ball frames | 1–3 weeks on an M2 (Apple's MPS backend, estimated). Start from the unofficial TranSPORTmer reimplementation in the PathCRF repository (MPL-2.0; the lab's own estimate is 4–6 days to adapt) |
| 2 | Structured damage: identity swaps inside a track piece, false or snapped ball detections, balls moving through stoppages, and more missing ball in dead time | Structured-fault robustness (NeurIPS 2025); training-time masking (2501.16767); the bench's listed gaps and the SkillCorner failure | Days; no licence issue |
| 3 | New labels: SoccerTrack v2 in training (dead ball, restarts), and ELASTIC or DataBallPy synchronisation to make touch-accurate possession on any clear-rights match | CC BY 4.0 data; ELASTIC MPL-2.0; DataBallPy MIT ([JOSS](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.10223)) | 1–2 weeks; check domain shift against SkillCorner first |
| 4 | Encode the 22 slots as a set (DeepSets/Set Transformer, or a Zoo-style home × away pairwise block) in front of the BiGRU. Add four-way mirror augmentation and an auxiliary ball head on frames where the ball is seen | Li & Link (the temporal model carries most of the signal); SoccerNet-GAR (small position-based graph networks win); NFL 2026 recipes; TacticAI symmetry. Two public competitor repos already use set encoders (`docs/field.md`) | 1–2 weeks; no licence issue (reimplementation) |
| 5 | T-MAE-style masked pretraining on unlabelled tracking (IDSSE, Metrica, SoccerTrack v2, and the competition match under RULES.md's self-supervision clause), then fine-tuning | Masking whole agents matches anonymous, broken tracks; multi-sport pretraining hurt, so stay with soccer | 2–4 weeks |
| 6 | Shift handling: EM prior correction applied with shrinkage only if it helps on SkillCorner; recalibration on *damaged* data; a regression that predicts spellF1 from agreement, confidence and spell-rate gaps | EM with bias-corrected temperature scaling is hard to beat but assumes the tracker does not change p(features \| label); label-free selection lags oracle selection | Days to 1 week |
| 7 | Decoder: a maximum length for dead-ball runs (CAD-style); decoding to maximise expected spellF1 (minimum Bayes risk), only once calibration holds under shift | CAD helps weak backbones only; no 2024–26 evidence for metric-aware decoding | Low priority |
| 8 | Uncertainty-aware completion (U2Diff, its heteroscedastic TPAMI extension, JointDiff) as a "don't know" signal for the decoder | Peer-reviewed, but these are diffusion models that need a GPU; code and licences not verified | Watch for code |

Several candidates should be dropped outright. Generic imputers (CSDI, SAITS, ImputeFormer) and time-series foundation models (MOMENT, plus TimesFM and Moirai, whose weights are reportedly non-commercial) cannot use the other agents' context. GRU-D and NCDE classifiers rank below gradient-boosted trees, entropy-based test-time adaptation fails on a single correlated match, and Mamba backbones have no published segmentation results. LTContext's and BaFormer's code is out for licence reasons, and UniTraj's weights for unclear data terms and CUDA-only layers. Google Research Football's synthetic data is tempting because its possession and game-mode labels come free, but the repository is archived and the gap between simulation and real tracking is large ([GRF docs](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/gfootball/doc/observation.md)). SoccerNet-GAR and SoccerNet's PCBAS data cannot serve as training data, since both are gated under "other" licences ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/SoccerNet/SN-PCBAS-2026)).

Item 1 carries one specific risk: data missing not at random. On the competition match the ball disappears in particular states (crowds, stoppages, near the lines). Artificially masked seen-ball frames are therefore not distributed like truly missing ones. Masks should follow the bench's state-dependent loss model, not uniform sampling.

## Conclusion

The research frontier and the team's real problem have drifted apart. Papers optimise ball-coordinate error or event mAP on clean, identity-consistent tracking. The team's score depends on segment F1 under a change of tracker, a quantity no 2025–2026 paper measures. That makes the bench, SkillCorner and the label-free checks more decision-relevant than any external result, and it raises the value of damage realism as research in its own right. The common thread in the stronger papers is that a missing ball becomes a routine training condition, not a gap to fill first. The pipeline already does a hand-built version of this (blanking still balls, damage matched to the target's gaps). The next step is to let a set encoder learn it from masked players, using the competition match's own seen-ball frames as rules-compliant self-supervision.

The gap in the dead-ball literature is also an opening. Even FIFA's own pipeline takes live/dead status from the data provider, so a learned dead-ball class plus a per-match switch for moving-ball stoppages has no published counterpart. A careful write-up could be a contribution to a venue such as MLSA rather than only a competition entry. For monitoring, the KAIST group behind PathCRF, ELASTIC and BallRadar is the one source that keeps releasing usable, licensed code in this niche. Watch its repositories first.

# The challenge, in plain words

Our own summary of the organisers' documents (README, CHALLENGE, DATA, EVALUATION, SUBMISSION,
RULES, FAQ in the repository root). If anything here disagrees with them, they are right.

## In one sentence

We get one full LaLiga match, FC Barcelona vs Athletic Club (2024-25, matchday 2), and must build a
program that says, for every frame of the video, which team has the ball or whether play is stopped.

## What we hand in

A CSV with a header `frame,label` and exactly **199,319** rows, one per frame of the tactical
video (25 frames per second, about 2 h 13 min including the time before kickoff and halftime).
Each row has one letter:

| label | meaning |
|---|---|
| `H` | home team (Barcelona) has the ball, ball in play |
| `A` | away team (Athletic) has the ball, ball in play |
| `D` | dead ball: out of play, foul, goal, waiting for a restart |
| `X` | no answer |

Only the two halves are graded: video frames **11,185–90,651** and **110,808–190,197**, which is
158,857 frames, about 106 minutes. Anything written outside them is ignored. The second half is graded
from 110,808 but kicks off at **113,742**, so its first 2,934 graded frames are all `D`.

## What counts as possession (from CHALLENGE.md)

- While the ball is in play, exactly one team has possession.
- Possession changes only when the other team gains **controlled** possession. A touch without
  control, a deflection or an aerial duel won without keeping the ball does **not** change it.
- While the ball is loose or contested, possession stays with the last team that had it.
- After every stoppage there is a clear team that restarts.
- A **possession** is a maximal run of the same team's label with the ball alive. `H H D D H H` is
  two possessions, not one.
- The official answers were derived mechanically from Metrica's **corrected** tracking: `D` where it
  marks the ball dead, otherwise the team it names as owning the ball.

## How we are scored

**Ranked on `spellF1`.** Each possession is one object. Our possessions are matched one-to-one with
the real ones by how much they overlap in time (overlap divided by combined span, "IoU"). A match
counts if the IoU passes a threshold, and the F1 of the matching is averaged over thresholds 0.3,
0.4, 0.5, 0.6 and 0.7. Constant or random answers score 0. Being 1 frame late is free, 1 second
late costs about a third (0.65).

**Tie-break: `macroF1`.** Per-frame F1 for H, A and D, averaged.

The two disagree on purpose. A method that flickers between teams gets many frames right (good
macroF1) but invents dozens of fake possessions (terrible spellF1). The baselines in the repo
show it:

| baseline | macroF1 (full match) | spellF1 (full match) |
|---|---|---|
| raw, nearest player to the ball | 0.6532 | 0.0486 |
| majority vote over ±1 s | 0.6752 | 0.1996 |
| **majority vote over ±2 s: the floor to beat** | 0.6724 | **0.2433** |
| majority vote over ±5 s | 0.6356 | 0.2242 |

Differences below ~0.04 spellF1 are noise on the full match.

**How `X` is scored.** macroF1 counts an `X` on a graded frame as wrong. spellF1 replaces each `X`
with our previous answer in the same half before matching. So `X` in the middle of a possession is
free, but `X` across a change of possession drags the old team forward.

## Rules that shape how we work

1. **Labels must come from an algorithm.** No one may label this match by hand, not even a few
   frames, not to train or to check a model. Hand labels on *other* matches are fine.
2. **The 5 public minutes are for checking our pipeline, not for tuning.** The repo shows that
   choosing a setting on them picks the wrong one for the full match.
3. **No human-made data about this match** from anywhere: Opta, StatsBomb, Wyscout, corrected
   tracking. External data about **other** matches is fine.
4. **The submitted file must be regenerated byte-for-byte** by running our public repository on
   the provided data.
5. **Don't publish the data.** Our repo will be public: `data/` stays out of git.
6. Data-terms clause about cloud LLMs: our reading, confirmed by Jose on 2026-10-30, is that it
   targets using an LLM to label frames. We inspect the data normally, and no LLM labels anything.
